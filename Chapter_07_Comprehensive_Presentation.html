Objections Are Good News

The Reframe That Changes Everything

What Amateurs Hear

  • "Too expensive" = rejection
  • "Let me think" = a no
  • Objections = conflict to avoid
  • Silence = success

What Professionals Hear

  • "Too expensive" = a value conversation invitation
  • "Let me think" = an unspoken question
  • Objections = a MAP of what stands between them and yes
  • No objections + no yes = polite avoidance

The prospect who objects is engaged. The one who vanishes quietly never was. Welcome objections — explore them, never argue with them. Every objection tells you exactly what to talk about next.

Our Negotiation Philosophy

Value-Based — and One Steady Hands Truth

Traditional Negotiation

  • Win-lose mentality
  • Price concessions as the main tool
  • Adversarial — pressure beats pressure
  • Short-term: close at any cost

Value-Based Negotiation

  • Win-win outcomes only
  • Value preserved and clarified — never discounted away
  • Collaborative: solve THEIR hesitation together
  • Long-term relationship over one sale

"The Price Is the Price." At $100 once and $20–30/month, we don't haggle — there's no discount culture here. Our negotiation isn't over dollars. It's over understanding: make the value so clear that the fixed price feels obvious."

— Steady Hands LLC

BATNA & ZOPA

The Mindset Behind Calm Confidence

BATNA — Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement

  • Their BATNA: free site builders, a nephew, another company — or doing nothing
  • Our BATNA: the next dial. 15–20 attempts an hour means a full pipeline always exists
  • Why it matters: you don't NEED this deal — you need the RIGHT deal
  • Desperation leaks through the phone. Calm closes.

ZOPA — Zone of Possible Agreement

  • For us it's not a price zone — it's a FIT zone
  • Their need + comfort + timing overlapping our offer
  • No overlap (score below 40 — Chapter 5)? There's no deal to force. Walk warmly.
  • Overlap exists? Then objections are just gaps in understanding — fill them with LACE

Know both BATNAs and you'll never sound desperate or arrogant. Calm confidence is the negotiation posture — everything else in this chapter rides on it.

LACE — The Objection Loop

Four Moves. In Order. Every Time.

L — ListenCompletely, without interruption
A — AcknowledgeValidate the person and the concern
C — ClarifyFind the REAL objection underneath
E — ExecuteRespond with value — to the real one
The failure pattern: most reps jump straight to "Answer" — skipping L, A, and C. The result: a brilliant response to the WRONG objection. The first objection stated is rarely the real one. LACE exists to find the real one — and the ORDER is the ethics: understand before you answer.

L — Listen Completely

The Step Everyone Skips — and Everyone Feels

What It Means

  • Let them finish — no interrupting, no finishing their sentences
  • Don't reload your comeback while they talk — listen to understand, not to reply
  • Let silence work — people elaborate into silence
  • Note the emotion under the words: frustration, fear, skepticism

What You're Listening For

  • The specific words they choose — they're your material for C and E
  • The worry beneath the objection ("burned before"? "monthly commitment"?)
  • What they DON'T say — the hesitation behind the hesitation
  • Whether it's one objection or several stacked

Full attention is rare — which makes it powerful. A prospect who feels genuinely heard lowers their guard. The close is won in the listening, not the talking.

A — Acknowledge

Validate the Person — Before Addressing the Point

The Phrases

  • "That's fair."
  • "That makes sense."
  • "I hear you — plenty of owners feel exactly that way."
  • "Thanks for being straight with me."

The Principle

  • Acknowledgment ≠ agreement — you validate the feeling without conceding facts
  • Defensiveness drops when people feel heard
  • A heard prospect can be guided; a dismissed one digs in
  • Anti-pattern: "Yes, BUT…" — the "but" erases everything before it. Pause or use "and" instead.

Ten seconds of acknowledgment buys you ten minutes of open conversation. Skip it, and every word of your brilliant answer bounces off a wall.

C — Clarify the Issue

The First Objection Is a Symptom — Find the Cause

The Clarifying Questions

  • "When you say expensive — compared to what?"
  • "What's the part you're most unsure about?"
  • "Suppose we solved that — is everything else a yes?" (the isolation test)
  • Usually takes 1–3 clarifications to reach the real objection

What "Too Expensive" Often Really Means

  • "I'm not yet convinced it'll actually help"
  • "I need my spouse's blessing"
  • "A previous web guy burned me"
  • "The monthly commitment worries me"

Answer the stated objection and you may win the point and lose the deal. Clarify until you're confident you've found the real one — then spend your E step there.

E — Execute / Establish Value

Now — and Only Now — You Answer

Your Tools

  • Their own numbers and the Gap (Chapters 4–6)
  • The preview process — risk removed before commitment
  • Plan flexibility — official: move between hosting levels anytime
  • Industry-matched real examples — cleaning, salons, landscaping

The Structure

  • Address the REAL objection specifically
  • Connect back to THEIR stated goal and Gap
  • Always close the loop with a check: "Does that address it?" or "Is that the concern, or is there more?"
  • New objection appears? New LACE loop. Loops — not lectures.
One loop at a time. Try to answer three objections in one breath and you'll answer none of them. Resolve, check, then move to the next.

LACE in Action

"Why would I pay $100 when Wix is free?"

L — LISTEN Let her finish: "…I looked at those free builders. Seems like everyone just uses them." No interrupting. No wincing.
A — ACKNOWLEDGE "That's fair — free is worth a serious look. And honestly, for some people it's the right call."
C — CLARIFY "Can I ask — when you picture your site, is it something you'd want to build and manage yourself, or something you want handled?" → "I don't have time to figure that stuff out. I'd want it handled." That's the real objection: not price — capacity.
E — EXECUTE "Then that's exactly the difference. Free builders hand you the tools — you become the designer, the mobile-fixer, the support desk. We're the hands: built for you, mobile-ready, preview before launch, and a real human at the end of the phone for $20 a month. If you'd enjoy building it yourself — genuinely, do the free route. If you want it handled — that's our $100." → "Does that make the difference clear?"
Notice: we respected the free alternative instead of trashing it. That honesty IS the persuasion.

Value & Price Objections

LACE'd Responses — Adapt, Don't Recite

"It's too expensive." "Fair to ask — compared to what? The free builders, or what you expected a site to cost? If it's the builders, the real comparison is your time — and who fixes it at 9pm. If it's expectations — most owners assume thousands. It's $100, once."
"What's the catch?" "No catch — the $100 covers the full design and build. You see a preview first, we customize before launch. Hosting starts at $20/month — that part IS monthly, and I'd rather tell you now than surprise you later."
"Can you do $50 and I'll sign today?" "The price is the price — $100 is what keeps real humans building and supporting it. What I can do is walk you through exactly what's included so you can judge the value yourself."
Discount requests are answered with VALUE, never with invented markdowns. The price protects the service — and your integrity.

Status Quo Objections

"I'm Fine As I Am" — The Quietest Deal-Killer

"I already have a website." "Perfect — then you know what it does for you. Quick question: when someone last pulled it up on their phone, how did it look? … Many owners we meet are due a refresh. A preview costs nothing but a conversation."
"Facebook is enough for me." "Facebook's great for people who already know you. The gap is the ones searching 'salon near me' who find whoever HAS a site. There you rent a page — here you'd own your address."
"My nephew handles that." "He might do a great job. The question is who's behind it at 9pm when it breaks — and who keeps it current on new phones. And if he's genuinely got it covered? Then that's fine — I'd rather you hear me say that than push. If you ever want it handled, we're one call away."
If the status quo is genuinely comfortable and TCI honest answers confirm it — that's a red-flag score (Ch 5). Qualify out respectfully. Forcing change where nothing hurts creates a resentful client.

Agenda Objections

Timing Pushback Gets Scheduled — Not Overcome

"It's not a good time." "Totally understandable — when IS a better window?" Log the date. "I'll call you then — and not before." Then honor it exactly.
"Let me think about it." LACE-C: "Of course. So I send you off with the right things to think about — which part deserves the thinking: the $100, the monthly, or whether it'll actually help?" "Thinking" is almost always one of those three — find it and answer the real question.
"Call me in November." (the landscaper) "November it is. Anything changes before then, you have my number." Log it. Return on time. That kept promise IS your reputation in that industry.
The rule: Agenda objections get SCHEDULED, not steamrolled. Forcing timing creates rushed, unhappy clients — and rushed clients churn, which costs more than the sale earned.

Authority Objections

"I Need to Ask My Spouse" — Bring Them In, Don't Sell Around Them

The Response

  • "Smart — it's a family decision." (acknowledge, never condescend)
  • "What's helped other owners: I send you the preview link — you show them in two minutes."
  • "Or if it's easier, let's get them on a quick call — I'd rather answer their questions myself than have you guess them."
  • Goal: the deciding conversation happens WITH us present — or well-armed

The Anti-Patterns

  • Never pressure a decision the person can't make alone
  • Never sell around the partner or go over their head
  • Never treat your contact as an obstacle — they're your internal ally
  • Never call back pressuring before the promised check-in time

Chapter 5 asked: "Who else needs to be involved?" — early. Chapter 7 answers what to do when the answer surfaces late: invite the circle in, arm your ally, and let the value case speak to everyone.

Trust Objections

"Who Are You Guys?" — Answer With Verifiable Facts, Never Insistence

"Never heard of you." "Fair — we're a Las Vegas web company, and I'm calling because businesses like yours are exactly who we serve. Everything's at steadyhandsop.com — and the process protects you: preview before anything goes live, and you can call or text us anytime at (702) 372-6399."
"How do I know you won't take the $100 and vanish?" "Because you see the actual site before launch — the preview is built first. And the monthly plan only continues if you're happy. Real number, real humans: (702) 372-6399 — call it right now if you like."
"Sounds too good to be true." "Then don't take my word for it — check the site, look at the pricing page, and decide. No pressure from me either way."
Trust objections are answered with transparency and things the prospect can VERIFY themselves — the official site, the official prices, the preview process, the real phone number. Insistence builds doubt; verifiability builds trust.

The Value Shield

When Pressure Arrives — Shield the Value, Never Cut the Price

The Shield's Four Anchors

  • Their quantified benefits: the Gap math they built with you (Ch 4–6)
  • Cost of inaction: their own words and numbers — not inflated fear
  • Value before price: restate what it does BEFORE price resurfaces
  • Official flexibility: preview before launch · move between plans anytime · support when needed

What the Shield Never Trades

  • The price itself — no invented discounts, ever
  • The honesty — no hiding the monthly commitment
  • The client's right to say no — pressure is banned
  • Your calm — BATNA mindset keeps tone steady

At our price point there's no haggle culture to enter. When someone pushes on price, the Shield's job is simple: move the conversation back to value until $100 + $20/month is obviously worth it — or it honestly isn't, and both of you know it.

The Banned List

Any One of These Is a Conduct Violation — No Exceptions

Never, Ever

  • Fake urgency or scarcity — "only today," "last spot"
  • Invented discounts or fake "let me ask my manager" theater
  • Disparaging competitors — or the client's nephew
  • Wearing them down — repeated calls after a clear no
  • Fear-selling — inflating consequences beyond their own stated math
  • Steamrolling — talking past objections instead of through them

Why the List Exists

  • One pressured client tells ten neighbors — reputation compounds in BOTH directions
  • Pressure "wins" create churn, refunds, andreferral death
  • The Mandate (Ch 1): grow through value, never through price — or pressure
  • Our small-market advantage IS the clean reputation — it's the moat

"We would rather lose a sale honestly than win one dishonestly. The first keeps the pipeline warm. The second burns the map."

— Steady Hands LLC

Buying Signals

When You Hear These — Move to the Close

The Signals

  • Process questions: "How long does it take?" "What do you need from me?"
  • Re-confirming details: "So I'd see a preview first?" — for the second time
  • Self-selling: "My customers keep asking if we have a site…"
  • Doing the math out loud: "So $100 once and $20 a month…"
  • Silence after your value statement — they're calculating. Don't fill it with chatter.

The Rule

  • Signal heard → ask for the next step — same call
  • Signals ignored go stale — momentum is perishable
  • Keep presenting PAST the signal and you re-open objections you'd already closed
  • When in doubt, a next-step close costs nothing (next slide)

The Consultative Close

Three Ethical Ways to Ask — and One Rule

1. THE SUMMARY CLOSE "You said new clients finding you matters most, the gap is those 4 a month, and the Standard plan covers it. Shall we get your preview started?" — Their goal + their gap + the fit, then the ask.
2. THE NEXT-STEP CLOSE — your default "Want me to get the preview built this week?" — Commits to a step, not a lifetime. Lowest pressure, highest honesty. Perfect for our preview-driven process.
3. THE DIRECT CLOSE — when signals are strong "Based on everything you've told me, I think this fits. Ready to start?" — Clear, confident, respectful.
The One Rule: ask ONCE, clearly — then STOP TALKING. Silence closes; chatter un-closes. If a real objection surfaces, run a LACE loop, resolve it, then close again. Never triple-ask, never guilt, never pounce on a pause.

After the Close — and After the "No"

Both Moments Define You

After YES

  • Confirm simply: what they chose, what happens next
  • Set expectations: preview → customize → launch
  • Log everything in the CRM — clean handoff to the team
  • Thank them like a partner, not a payday — the Flywheel (next chapter) starts HERE

After NO

  • Thank them genuinely — for their time and honesty
  • Leave one useful insight behind — value even in a no
  • Window exists? Schedule it. No window? Door open: "Anytime — (702) 372-6399."
  • Log it honestly. "No" means "not now" — the scorecard decides when to return

How you behave at "yes" decides the relationship. How you behave at "no" decides the referral. Owners remember the rep who was graceful in both.

Chapter 7 — Summary

What You Must Walk Away With

The Loop

  • LACE — Listen · Acknowledge · Clarify · Execute — in order, every time
  • Objections are information — a map, not a fight
  • The Map: Value · Status Quo · Agenda · Authority · Trust
  • Clarify to the REAL objection — 1–3 loops deep

The Line

  • The price is the price — the Value Shield holds it with value
  • The Banned List — zero pressure tactics, zero fake urgency
  • Close once, clearly, then stop talking
  • Respect every "no" — schedule windows, keep doors open

Quick Check

Test Yourself Before Moving On

1. The prospect just said "that seems like a lot for a website." In LACE order, your FIRST move is:

Explain everything included in the $100 Listen completely — let them finish, without interrupting or reloading your answer Offer to walk them through the preview process

L comes before everything. Understanding precedes answering — that's the ethics AND the effectiveness of the loop.

2. A client says: "Let me think about it." The best LACE-C clarification is:

"What's there to think about? It's only $100." "Of course. So I send you off with the right things to think about — which part deserves the thinking: the $100, the monthly, or whether it'll actually help?" "Okay — I'll call you next month!" (hang up)

"Thinking about it" hides a real question. Clarify to find it — it's almost always the price, the commitment, or the doubt about value.

3. A prospect pushes: "Do it for $70 and I'll sign today." You:

Agree — a closed deal at $70 beats no deal at $100 Say "Let me ask my manager for a special discount" and pretend to check Hold the line: "The price is the price — $100 is what keeps real humans building and supporting it. Let me show you exactly what's included so you can judge the value yourself."

The price is the price. The Value Shield responds with value — never invented discounts, never fake approval theater.
